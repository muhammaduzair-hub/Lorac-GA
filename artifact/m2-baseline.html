What was built

A self-contained FedAvg simulation on HuggingFace transformers and PEFT — Route B, with FederatedScope-LLM kept as an architectural reference rather than a runtime dependency. One model instance is reused across all simulated clients; only adapter tensors are swapped, so memory does not grow with K.

Rounds 5–10 cost 355 MB — 60% of the run's entire budget — and bought 1.15 accuracy points over round 4's 84.06%. Under the cost model C = R·K·r·s₀, that gap is the thesis question in miniature.

The round-3 dip was data, not a defect

Accuracy fell from 68.8% to 61.1% at round 3. Summing the shard sizes of each round's ten sampled clients explains it: round 3 drew the smallest pool of the entire run, round 4 the second largest.

Training samples available per round

sum of the ten selected clients' shards

Under Dirichlet skew, which ten clients get drawn matters as much as how many. That is precisely the tension the genetic algorithm has to navigate in M4 — and a reason client count alone may be an incomplete decision variable.

The split is genuinely non-IID

Dirichlet α=0.3 across 100 clients produced shards spanning 1 to 4,733 samples. 67 of 100 clients hold at least 90% of a single class, so the baseline is not quietly training on near-IID data.

Two findings that shape M3 and M4

Carries into M3

Saturation arrives long before the budget is spent

Rounds 1–4 delivered 84.06%; rounds 5–10 added 1.15 points for 355 MB. If accuracy saturates in R the way the paper expects it to in K, then treating R as fixed at 10 may be leaving budget on the table. Worth raising before the A(K, r) surface is profiled.

Carries into M4

Client identity, not just client count

A 3.7× swing in available samples between rounds moved accuracy by 23 points. The GA optimises K under a budget, but two draws of the same K are not equivalent under α=0.3. Expect variance across seeds — which is exactly why M5 runs more than one.
